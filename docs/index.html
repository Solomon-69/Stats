What it does

Your Mac, at a glance

Stats sits in the menu bar and keeps the health of your Mac one click away. It is free and open source, and it speaks plainly.

Plain words, not jargon

Each tile says what is going on in everyday words, like “Plenty of room”, “Mostly resting” or “3 h 10 min left”, and opens up for more detail.

Live tiles

Processor, graphics, memory, storage, disk activity, internet, battery, temperature, fans and Bluetooth gadgets, with small graphs that fill in the moment you open the panel. Tap a tile to expand it and the grid stays neat.

Honest numbers

Battery time comes from the real power draw, battery health matches System Settings, and temperatures come from the processor sensors.

Your Mac at the top

The panel starts with your Mac’s model name and its chip, cores, memory and macOS version. A short note appears beside it when something needs attention, like memory getting full. Fans and Battery tiles hide themselves on Macs that have none. A short welcome tour opens on first launch, and you can add live readouts such as CPU or memory as extra menu bar items. Pick which tile opens by itself, and show every temperature sensor at full size.

A menu bar icon your way

Choose Bars, Chip, Pulse, a live graph of any reading, or hide the icon. It can turn green, amber or red with how hard your Mac is working.

Light, dark and your units

Light, dark or automatic. Pick °C or °F and MB/s or Mbps, and choose how often the numbers refresh. Pick an accent colour for the Settings screens too.

Alerts when it matters

Turn on alerts for low battery, full storage or squeezed memory.

Opens at login, updates itself

Open Stats at login so it is always there. It updates itself from the releases on GitHub.

Install

Install Stats

Download it, open it, drag it into Applications. Stats is signed and checked by Apple, so it opens like any normal app.

Mac

macOS 14 Sonoma or later

Download for Mac Stats.dmg

Or paste this into Terminal. It downloads, installs and opens Stats in one go.

curl -fsSL https://solomon-69.github.io/Mac-Apps/download/install.sh | bash -s stats
  1. Open the downloaded file and drag Stats into Applications.
  2. Open Stats from Applications. Look for the Stats icon in the menu bar and click it.
  3. Tap any tile to see more. Open Settings in the panel to choose your menu bar icon, units and alerts.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit

Download for Windows Stats-windows.zip

Prefer an installer? Stats-Setup.exe does the same.

  1. Unzip the download and open Stats.exe. Look for the Stats icon in the tray, bottom right, and click it.
  2. Windows may show a blue screen because the app is not signed yet: choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Tap any tile to see more. Open Settings in the panel to choose units and alerts.

Questions

Good to know

Does Stats send my information anywhere?

Stats reads the numbers from your own Mac to draw its tiles. The one online thing it does is update itself from the releases on GitHub.

What does Stats show?

Live tiles for the processor, graphics, memory, storage, disk activity, internet, battery, temperature, fans and Bluetooth gadgets. Each one says what is going on in everyday words and opens up for more detail.

Can I change the menu bar icon?

Yes. Choose Bars, Chip, Pulse, a live graph of any reading, or hide it. It can turn green, amber or red with how hard your Mac is working.

Can I use Fahrenheit?

Yes. In Settings you can switch between °C and °F, and between MB/s and Mbps.

Does it work on every Mac?

Stats needs macOS 14 or later. It was built and tried on an Apple silicon MacBook Air, so fan readings and sensors on Intel Macs have not been tested yet.

Can I build it myself?

Yes. Stats is open source under the MIT licence. The code and build steps are on GitHub.

Opening the app

Stats is signed and checked by Apple (notarized), so it opens like any normal app: open the download, drag to Applications, double-click. You can check a download against the SHA-256 fingerprints in each release (the SHA256SUMS file) on GitHub.
